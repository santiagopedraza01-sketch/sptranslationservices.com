Sworn vs certified translation: which one do you need?

Guide · Updated 14 October 2026

“Certified”, “sworn”, “official” and “apostilled” are often used as if they meant the same thing. They do not. Here is what each one is and how to know which one your institution expects.

Guide

Standard translation

An accurate translation with no signature or certificate. It is right for understanding a document, internal company use, or anything that is not being submitted to an authority.

Certified translation

An official translation issued, signed, dated and stamped by a professional, accredited translator, who takes responsibility for it being a complete and faithful translation of the original. This is what most institutions in Malta, the UK, Ireland and many other countries mean when they ask for an “official” translation.

Sworn translation

In some countries, only translators appointed by the state can produce translations with legal validity. In Spain, for example, a traducción jurada must be signed and stamped by a traductor-intérprete jurado appointed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Other countries, including Italy and parts of Latin America, have their own systems of sworn or public translators.

If your document is going to Spain, tell us before you order. We will confirm whether the institution requires a sworn translation and how to obtain it.

Apostilled translation

An apostille confirms the authenticity of a public document for use in another country. It can be placed on the original document, and in some systems on the translation too. Whether the translation also needs an apostille depends on the destination country. See how to apostille documents from Colombia, Mexico and Spain.

Quick guide

  • For your own information: standard translation.
  • For an authority, employer, bank or university in Malta: certified translation.
  • For an authority in Spain: usually a sworn translation (traducción jurada).
  • For an authority in another country: often a certified translation of an apostilled original, but always check the local rules.

Requirements change and differ between authorities. Always check the current rules with the institution that will receive your documents, and ask us if anything is unclear before you order.
