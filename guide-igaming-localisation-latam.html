iGaming localisation for Latin America: 10 things to get right

Guide · Updated 14 October 2026

Latin America is not one market, and “Spanish” is not one language variant. A practical checklist for operators and affiliates preparing to launch.

Guide
  1. Choose neutral or local Spanish per content type. Neutral Latin American Spanish works for help centres and legal text; marketing, promotions and sportsbook copy usually perform better when adapted to each country.
  2. Do not reuse Spain’s Spanish. Vocabulary such as ordenador or vosotros immediately signals “foreign brand” to Latin American players.
  3. Decide on voseo for Argentina. Argentine players use vos rather than tú. Using it makes a casual brand feel local; a formal brand may prefer usted.
  4. Localise betting vocabulary. Odds are momios in Mexico and cuotas elsewhere; terms for accumulators and parlays also vary. Build a glossary per market.
  5. Lead with local sport. Liga MX, Liga BetPlay, the Argentine Primera División and the Copa Libertadores drive far more engagement than European leagues alone.
  6. Show local payment methods. OXXO and SPEI in Mexico, PSE and Nequi in Colombia, Mercado Pago across the region, Pix in Brazil. The cashier copy has to name them correctly.
  7. Format currency and numbers locally. Peso symbols, thousand separators and decimal commas differ between countries, and errors in bonus amounts create support tickets.
  8. Treat compliance text as its own project. Responsible gambling messages, age limits and T&Cs depend on each market’s regulator; translate precisely and flag where a separate version is needed.
  9. Research keywords per country. Casino online and casino en línea do not perform equally everywhere. Translate your SEO strategy, not just your pages.
  10. Brazil is Portuguese, not Spanish. Use native Brazilian translators and a separate glossary; adapted Spanish will not pass.
