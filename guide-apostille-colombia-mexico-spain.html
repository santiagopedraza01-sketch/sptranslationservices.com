How to apostille documents from Colombia, Mexico and Spain

Guide · Updated 14 October 2026

An apostille confirms that a public document is genuine so it can be used in another country. Here is who issues it in Colombia, Mexico and Spain, and how it fits with your translation.

Guide

What is an apostille?

An apostille is a certificate added to a public document, such as a birth certificate, diploma or court judgment, that confirms the signature and seal on it are genuine. It was created by the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, and it replaces the longer chain of legalisation through embassies between member countries. Malta, Spain, Mexico, Colombia and most Latin American countries are members.

The apostille is always issued in the country where the document was issued. A Colombian birth certificate is apostilled in Colombia, not in Malta.

Colombia

Apostilles are issued by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Cancillería), mostly online through its apostille and legalisation service. The signature of the official who signed your document must be registered with the Cancillería; for some documents, such as academic records or documents signed by private entities, an extra step may be needed first. The apostille is issued electronically and can be verified online.

Mexico

Who apostilles depends on who issued the document:

  • Federal documents (for example, documents from federal agencies or federal courts) are apostilled by the Secretaría de Gobernación (SEGOB).
  • State documents, including birth and marriage certificates from the Civil Registry and most state university documents, are apostilled by the government of the state that issued them, usually through its legalisation and apostille office.

A birth certificate from Jalisco is therefore apostilled in Jalisco, even if you live in Mexico City.

Spain

  • Civil registry, judicial and many administrative documents are apostilled by the Ministry of Justice, which offers an electronic apostille, or by the regional High Courts of Justice.
  • Notarial documents, such as powers of attorney, are apostilled by the regional College of Notaries.
  • University degrees may need prior recognition of the signature before the apostille; check with your university.

Apostille first, then translate

In most cases, the best order is:

  1. Get a recent original or official copy of the document.
  2. Apostille it in the issuing country.
  3. Have the document and the apostille translated together, so the receiving authority can read both.

Some destinations also ask for the translation itself to be apostilled or sworn. This depends on the country where you will present it, so tell us the destination when you request a quote and we will check before you pay.

Requirements change and differ between authorities. Always check the current rules with the institution that will receive your documents, and ask us if anything is unclear before you order.
