Malta Single Permit 2026: the documents you need translated, and how to avoid delays

Moving to Malta · Updated 20 November 2026 · 7 min read

A practical checklist for non-EU workers and their employers: what goes into a Single Permit application, which papers need an English translation, and the 2026–2027 changes to plan around.

Key facts at a glance
  • The Single Permit is Malta’s combined work and residence permit for non-EU nationals with a job offer from a Malta-registered employer.
  • Any document that is not in English or Maltese, such as a diploma, transcript or reference letter, needs a translation into English (or Maltese).

If you are a non-EU national with a job offer in Malta, the Single Permit is the permit that lets you live and work here. Most applications that stall do so for the same few reasons: a document that is not in English, names spelled differently across papers, an expired certificate, or a qualification that has not been recognised yet. This guide explains what goes into the application, which documents you will need translated and how to prepare them so your file is complete the first time.

What the Single Permit is

The Single Permit combines the work licence and the residence permit in one application. It is for third-country nationals (citizens of countries outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland) who have been offered a job by a company registered and operating in Malta. The application is filed with the immigration authorities in Malta, usually with the employer’s involvement, and the permit is tied to that employer and position.

Processing runs from the date the file is complete. Missing pages, unreadable scans or untranslated documents restart the clock, which is why preparing documents properly matters as much as the application itself.

Documents in a Single Permit application

The exact list depends on your role, nationality and employer, and the authorities can ask for more. These are the documents most applicants prepare, according to the official guidance at the time of writing:

Some routes and later applications, such as family reunification, also ask for birth and marriage certificates or a police conduct certificate. If yours are in another language, translate them at the same time to save a second round.

Which documents need a translation

The rule of thumb is simple: anything that is not in English or Maltese needs a translation into English (or Maltese). For most Single Permit applicants that means:

  • Degree certificates, diplomas and vocational certificates
  • Academic transcripts and mark sheets
  • Reference letters, experience certificates and payslips from previous employers
  • Civil status documents (birth or marriage certificates) when your route or a later application requires them
  • Police conduct certificates, when requested

A translation for official use should be complete (every stamp, seal, signature note and handwritten annotation is rendered), laid out like the original, and issued, signed, dated and stamped by a professional, accredited translator. Partial or summary translations are a common reason for documents to be queried.

Documents with an apostille: translate the apostille too. The authority will see both, so the translation should cover everything on the page.

Apostille or legalisation first?

Public documents issued abroad usually need to be authenticated in the country that issued them before they are used in Malta:

  • Hague Apostille Convention countries (for example India, the Philippines, Colombia and most of Latin America): the issuing country adds an apostille.
  • Non-Hague countries: the document is usually legalised through the issuing country’s foreign ministry and the relevant embassy or consulate.

The usual order is: obtain the document, apostille or legalise it in the issuing country, then have the document and its apostille translated together. Our apostille guide walks through the process for Colombia, Mexico and Spain, and the same logic applies to other Hague countries.

Getting your qualifications recognised (MQRIC)

Many Single Permit applications need proof that your qualification has been recognised in Malta, or at least that you have applied. Recognition is handled by MQRIC, part of the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA). For a qualification that is not yet on its database, the application asks for scans of the original certificates plus translations into English or Maltese. MFHEA lists fees of €15 for a qualification already on the MQRIC database and €30 for one that is not.

Start this early: recognition can take time, and translating your degree and transcript once, for both MQRIC and the permit, avoids paying twice.

The Pre-Departure Course (Skills Pass)

Since 2026, non-EU nationals applying to work in Malta for the first time must complete a mandatory Pre-Departure Course before applying for the Single Permit. It is run through the Skills Pass platform (skillspass.org.mt). Reports at the time of writing describe:

  • a fee of €250, paid through the Skills Pass portal;
  • online modules on living and working in Malta and on workplace rights and obligations, followed by a short assessment interview;
  • verification of the course certificate as part of the permit process, with sector-specific requirements for tourism and hospitality, the only “claimed sector” so far, where workers also need a valid Skills Pass.

Build the course into your timeline: allow several weeks between registering and filing the permit application.

Budget 2027: what may change from January

Malta’s Budget 2027, presented on 26 October 2026, announced higher costs for third-country nationals. As reported in the press, the measures include raising the first-time application fee from €600 to €800 and introducing an annual €400 infrastructure contribution for new applicants and renewals (not for minors), from January 2027. It also announced a monthly charge for driving in Malta on a non-EU category B licence.

Fees and start dates take effect only once the implementing legal notices are published, and reports differ on some details. If your application is close to the deadline, check the current fee with the authorities or your employer before you file. Getting your translations ready early lets you apply as soon as you are eligible.

A realistic timeline

  1. Weeks 1–2: collect documents; request recent civil or police certificates if needed; apostille or legalise them in your country.
  2. Week 2: send everything for translation at once (standard turnaround is 24–48 hours per certificate).
  3. Weeks 2–6: complete the Pre-Departure Course; apply for MQRIC recognition.
  4. Then: the employer and you file the Single Permit application. Processing typically takes weeks to a few months from a complete file.

Common mistakes that delay applications

  • Names that don’t match. Your name should be spelled the same way as in your passport on every translation. Tell your translator how it appears.
  • Translating before the apostille. The translation then misses the apostille, and you pay twice.
  • Missing pages or the back of a certificate. Scan both sides, including stamps and annotations.
  • Old certificates. Civil status and police certificates may need to be recent; check the validity asked for.
  • Unofficial translations. Translations by friends or online tools are usually not accepted for official use.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to translate my passport for a Malta Single Permit?

Usually not. Passport data pages already include English. Documents fully in another language, such as diplomas, transcripts, references and civil or police certificates, need an English translation.

Can I translate my documents myself?

For official applications, translations should be done by a professional translator and issued, signed, dated and stamped, not by the applicant, a friend or an online tool. Self-made translations are a common reason for documents to be queried.

Should I apostille my documents before or after translation?

Before. Have the original apostilled or legalised in the issuing country first, then translate the document and the apostille together, so the translation covers everything the authority will see.

How long does a certified translation take?

With SP Translations, a standard certificate takes 24–48 hours once we receive a clear scan. A same-day or next-morning priority service is available for an extra 30%, subject to availability.

Do I need MQRIC recognition before applying?

Many applications ask for MQRIC recognition of your qualification, or proof that you have applied. MQRIC asks for translations of certificates that are not in English or Maltese, so translate your diploma and transcript once and use them for both.

How much will the Single Permit cost in 2027?

Budget 2027 announced a higher first-time fee and a yearly infrastructure contribution from January 2027, but the amounts apply only once the legal notices are published. Check the current fee with the authorities or your employer before filing.
